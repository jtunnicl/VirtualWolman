1 · The field book

Looking through the level, you see three horizontal lines (hairs) on the staff. For each staff position the group booked all three readings, in metres.

  • Middle reading → how far the ground is below the level's line of sight. A bigger number means lower ground.
  • Upper − lower → how far away the staff is (Step 3).
  • Station 0 is the staff held right next to the level. The transect runs from the top of the beach down to the water's edge.

Field book

2 · Check the readings

The middle hair sits exactly halfway between the upper and lower hairs, so the middle reading should equal the average of the other two. This is how surveyors catch reading or booking mistakes in the field.

3 · Work out the distances

The gap between the upper and lower hairs looks bigger on a close staff and smaller on a far one. The level is built so that distance = 100 × (upper − lower). This is called stadia distance.

4 · Work out the heights

The level's line of sight is perfectly flat, so the difference between two middle readings is the difference in ground height. We measure every height from the water's edge, so the water's edge is 0 m.
