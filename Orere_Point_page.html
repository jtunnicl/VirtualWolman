Stop A1/B6 · Orere Point

Orere Point is where the Orere River reaches the Firth of Thames. In the field this stop had two tasks: sketch the cliff west of the car park, and measure the gravel on the upper beach ridge. You will do both here from photos, using two online tools.

1. Look around

Open the 360° panorama of Orere Point in Google Maps (opens in a new tab). Drag to look around. Find the river mouth, the gravel spit, the beach ridge and the cliff.

2. What's going on here

The Orere River drains a 39 km² catchment in the north-east Hunua Ranges. The catchment is built of greywacke and argillite (mudstone) that weathers to a yellow-brown, clay-rich material. In the lower reaches, river terraces stand up to about 30 m above sea level; some are relics of Pleistocene high sea levels.

The cliff just west of the car park exposes several layers (units) of sediment. Your job is to work out what they are and how they got there.

3. Task A — the cliff section

Open the cliff section logger (opens in a new tab), then work through its steps:

  1. Scale: the person standing in front of the cliff is about 1.65 m tall. Click the top of her head, then her feet.
  2. Boundaries: click the base of the bare cliff face, then each boundary between layers, working upwards.
  3. Describe: for each unit, choose the sediment type and add its colour and anything else you notice (layering, roundness of the stones, roots).
  4. Hand in: print or save the page as a PDF. This is your annotated sketch with a vertical scale.

The person is in front of the cliff, closer to the camera, so the heights you get are minimum estimates.

Q3: Were the gravels exposed in the cliff transported and deposited by a stream/alluvial fan from the Hunua Ranges? Provide reasons for your answer.

Q4: If these gravel layers are fluvially emplaced and were graded to sea-level, what might this tell us about the changes that have occurred at this site?

4. Task B — sediment on the upper beach ridge

In the field you would measure 25 clasts with callipers. Here you will sample photos of the upper beach ridge with a grid. There are three photos — your lecturer may ask you to do one, or share them across a group.

  1. Measure stone size — set the scale on the lens cap (84 mm across), make a grid, and measure the a- and b-axis of the stone under each point. Step 6 gives the mean clast size and the D-values.
  2. Identify what's there — on the same photo, say whether each grid point sits on greywacke, other rock, shell, sand or plant material. Step 5 gives the % of gravel clasts and other material.

Your scale and grid are shared between the two, so you only set them up once per photo.

Q: Would you expect the beach gravel to be dominated by greywacke or other rock types? Why?

What to put in your report for this stop

  • The cliff printout (as your annotated sketch), plus answers to Q3 and Q4.
  • Mean clast size (mm) of the upper beach ridge, saying which axis you used.
  • % gravel clasts vs other material.
  • Your answer to the greywacke question.
